# Predicting the Risk of Public Works Suspension

## Project Description

This thesis proposal aims to develop and evaluate artificial intelligence models to estimate, in advance, the probability that a public works project in Peru will be suspended due to contractual non-compliance. The study focuses on transforming administrative records from official sources—primarily INFObras, the Invierte.pe Investment Bank, and SEACE—into useful information for identifying and prioritizing projects that may require preventive attention.

The research will follow an applied, quantitative, and predictive approach. A reproducible pipeline will be built to integrate and prepare the data, define risk factors, and train interpretable statistical models and machine learning algorithms. Given the imbalance between suspended and non-suspended projects, the comparison will consider metrics suited to minority events, such as *Recall*, F2, and PR-AUC, as well as probability calibration. The evaluation will preserve the temporal order of the data to approximate prospective model use and reduce the risk of information leakage.

Finally, explainable artificial intelligence techniques will be used to examine risk factors both across the project population and in individual cases. The expected outcome is an analytical dataset and an interpretable predictive model to support supervision and preventive decision-making. The model is intended to complement—not replace—the technical, legal, and administrative assessments conducted by public agencies.

## General Objective

To develop, compare, and evaluate artificial intelligence models that estimate the risk of public works suspension due to contractual non-compliance using official records, with attention to class imbalance, temporal evaluation, probability calibration, and interpretability.
